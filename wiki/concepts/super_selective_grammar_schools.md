## Definition
Super-selective grammar schools are state-funded secondary schools that admit pupils based on high academic ability, typically determined by entrance exams (e.g., the 11-plus). They admit a very small percentage of the highest-scoring students in a wide geographical area.

## Why it matters (in Poovi's context)
The concentration of these schools in and around Tonbridge makes the town a highly strategic location for families seeking top-tier education for academically gifted children, significantly driving demand for housing.

## Key properties or components
- Academic selection
- High entry standards
- State-funded
- Limited places
- Wide catchment area

## Contradictions or debates
The intense competition for places can create stress for students and parents, and the focus on academic selection may not suit all learning styles or developmental needs.
