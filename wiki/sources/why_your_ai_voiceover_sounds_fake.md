## Summary
This video explains that AI voiceovers often sound fake due to a mismatch between the voice's perfection and the video's quality, not the voice itself. It offers a three-step solution: using ElevenLabs' V3 model with a 'creative' stability setting, applying low-quality phone and small room audio filters, and writing scripts with short, present-tense sentences that describe performable actions. The creator also advises cloning only voices one has the right to use, recommending one's own voice for audience trust.

## Key claims
- The primary reason AI voiceovers sound fake is the mismatch between a 'studio perfect' voice and imperfect video footage.
- Using ElevenLabs' V3 model with a higher 'creative' stability setting introduces subtle imperfections that make the voice sound more natural.
- Applying audio filters like 'low-quality phone' and 'small room' enhances realism by simulating a real-world recording environment.
- Scriptwriting for AI video generation should focus on short, present-tense sentences describing single, performable actions to align with the video model's capabilities.
- It is crucial to only clone voices that you have the legal right to use, with one's own voice being the safest option.

## Contradictions or open questions
The video challenges the common belief that AI voiceovers sound fake solely due to the voice model itself, proposing that the mismatch with video quality is the primary culprit.
