## Summary
This video discusses the environmental impact of large dams, arguing that they disrupt natural water systems and ecological balance. It draws parallels to Darwin's theory of evolution, suggesting that altering the environment to this extent makes it difficult for current life forms to adapt. The speaker highlights that dam construction, once seen as progress, is now often driven by profit, benefiting only a small percentage of the population rather than overall societal development.

## Key claims
- Large dams disrupt the natural functioning of rivers and ecosystems, potentially leading to negative consequences.
- The Earth's biosphere relies on a delicate balance of elements like nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon dioxide, which can be upset by human interventions.
- Humanity's ability to survive is linked to adapting to environmental changes, as suggested by Darwin's theory of evolution.
- Historically, dams were viewed as symbols of progress, but modern trends, particularly post-globalisation, see them as profit-driven ventures.
- The benefits of such large-scale infrastructure projects are often concentrated among a small elite, rather than contributing to the overall development of a region.
